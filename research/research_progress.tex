\paragraph{Exceptional Gram bulk and remainder.}
The exact zero Poisson frequency of a block is
\[
 \operatorname{Bulk}_{C,d,k}
 =\frac43\mu_{C,d,k}N^2\int_{\mathbb C^2}W.
\]
Lean proves this split, the annular nonzero-frequency estimate, and
the supported-ideal summation. With the stated arithmetic block inputs,
the remainder satisfies the paper-level bound
\[
 |E_{\rm exc}-E_{\rm bulk}|
 \ll \operatorname{height}^{J}\frac Q{Y'}
          P^{1/3}Y'^{-1/6}Z^\rho,\qquad P=Y'^2/Q.
\]
It is at most the diagonal scale up to $Z^\rho$ when $P^2\le Y'$.
Replacing the coefficient by its complete mean inside the lattice sum
would not give this exact remainder.

The local arithmetic mean vanishes unless the combined valuations of
$C(k)$ are divisible by six away from fixed support.
The surviving common-part sum has a positive local Euler factor.
There are explicit paper-level exceptional families with positive complete mean,
including the actual calibration and fixed ray restrictions.
Their calibration spectrum and CRT assembly are not yet formalized.
Thus calibration does not annihilate every exceptional arithmetic block.
For one specified sixth-power frequency family, the actual finite pools
can be replaced by Euler products with a proved error. Its weighted
bulk has leading order
\[
 c_{S,k_0}\frac QY P^{1/6}
 |M_W(1/6+iv)|^2M_{F_U}(1/6),\qquad c_{S,k_0}>0.
\]
Here $U$ is the Gram energy test. For nonnegative nonzero $U$ and fixed
$v$ with $M_W(1/6+iv)\ne0$, the coefficient is positive.
The absolute error is
\[
 O\!\left(\frac QY\left[P^{1/12}+1+
       \frac{P^{(1+\eta)/6}+1}{Y}\right]\right).
\]
This is a reviewed paper-level asymptotic for one family.
It does not settle cancellation of the full weighted bulk.
See \texttt{gram\_bulk.tex} and

\paragraph{Direct mixed Gram estimates.}
The actual calibrated complete mean is zero. The following block bounds
are paper-level analytic deductions. Lean checks the local algebra and
the nonzero Fourier tail used in their proofs.
For squarefree source
ideals, a dyadic mixed block with source norm $Y$, completed-column
norm $C$, cubic-divisor norm $N$, and $CN^3\asymp T$ satisfies
\[
 |\mathcal M_{C,N,H\le H_0}|
 \ll (1+|v|)^J T^\rho
       \min\{\sqrt{CH_0},H_0/\sqrt Y\}.
\]
Here $H$ is the residual primitive conductor and the constants use the
fixed smooth windows and $Y\ll T$.
This proves a saving against $Q/\sqrt Y$ for $H_0=Q/P^\delta$.
The full block, including nonsquarefree additive sources, satisfies
\[
 |\mathcal M_{C,N}|
 \ll (1+|v|)^J T^\rho C\sqrt{YN}
 = (1+|v|)^J T^\rho\frac{T\sqrt Y}{N^{5/2}}.
\]
It saves $P^{-\delta}$ against $Q/\sqrt Y$ when
$N\gg(TYP^\delta/Q)^{2/5}$.
At the original geometry and $\delta=0$, this threshold has exponent
$13/40$ for $N$, or $23/120$ for $C$.
The nonsquarefree reduction retains the exact prime-power Gauss support
and sums a convergent squareful-ideal weight. The $\sqrt{CH_0}$ branch
also extends to all additive sources. The stronger $H_0/\sqrt Y$ branch
is currently proved only for squarefree sources.
These absolute estimates are uniform in the real inverse height, which
occurs only in a unit-modulus coefficient.
The remaining squarefree-source range has large $H$ and small $N$.
These are raw mixed-sum estimates. The final probe has an additional
$Q^{-1/2}$ normalization and a Mellin integral.
They do not imply a bound relative to the actual completed-row norm.
See \texttt{gram\_mixed.tex} for the exact coefficients and the current
scope of the nonsquarefree-source reduction.
